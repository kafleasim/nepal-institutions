# Political Settlements Analysis 

This follows Political Settlements and Development, by Tim Kelsall, Nicolai Schulz, William Ferguson, Matthias vom Hau, Sam Hickey and Brian Levy, OUP 2022.

They integrate several politcal models and ideas that I reall was enamored with, into a framework that is meant to be useful for practitioners. In this way, it is directly in line with the work that we are trying to do on integrating political economy and institutional thinking into growth diagnostic projects. 

First, they divide the polity into three blocs:

- The leaders block (LB) is the segment of the population that the current leader can be assured of. 
- The contingently loyal bloc (CLB) is the segment that is currently aligned but could defect realistically
- the opposition bloc (OB) is the segment of the population that is not currently aligned with LB or CLB

So you can have a ruling coalition that in addition to facing external opposition can be internally fractured. 

They create a typology to describe political settlements - a political settlement is an agreement among powerful groups that maintains peace and stability. 


For power, they develop the 'power concentration index'. The power concentration index is the aggregate of two sub-indices: vertical power and horizontal power 

    Horizontal power has to do with how much 'holding power' the ruling coalition has vs the opposition. 
        It is basically, how strong is the opposition bloc. 

    Vertical power is more complicated - it involves the relationship between the 
        leadership bloc and the contingent leadership bloc, 
        and the cohesion of the leadership bloc itself. 

For social foundation, the question is what percentage of the population is potentially disruptive, and co-opted. They have key groups: those that are potentially disruptive that are co-opted; groups with disruptive potential that are repressed ; and groups that dont have disruptive potential (marginal). 

They arrive at a number by asking: what pop share is represented by LB, CLB and OB - what percent of the social groups w/in it is powerful; and what percent of this bloc is co-opted vs repressed. Then you can work out a score by doing the share of population * powerful ppl * cooption-scale. 

        How can someone in the opposition bloc be co-opted?? wouldnt that put them in clb? they have a sentence: "even co-opted groups may be part of the political opposition, especially in political democracies". 


Implications for development? In many ways, society is tasked with resolving collective action problems. 

Concentrated settlements have an advantage for two potential reasons:

    1. political elites are more likely to have long time-horizons (stationary bandits), 
        - so potentially higher rates of investment over consumption, 
        - more productive vs less productive forms of rent-seeking, 
        - elites get the space to build disciplined state capacity needed to enable technological learning and upgrading. 
        - maybe more closely mapped by 'horizontal power' metric

    2. the ability to enforce growth policy on potential losers, whether its rent-seeking elites or expropriated popular classes
        - maybe more closely mapped by 'vertical' power


A broad social foundation means elites incentivized to try and promote more inclusive growth patterns. Maybe less rapid growth than a narrow foundation, but more equitable, less expropriation and dispossesion. Narrow social foundations can still have inclusive growth, but more likely as an externality or trickle-down effect.
