# Political Economy and Growth Diagnostics

A walk through the frameworks behind the country profile

---

# Three forces matter for growth

- **Politics** — the distribution of power, the contest over rents and authority
- **Institutions** — the durable rules that shape behavior
- **State capacity** — the ability to implement, regulate, and enforce

And they don't sit in separate boxes. Politics shapes which institutions take hold; institutions shape what the state can actually do; state capacity loops back to who holds power tomorrow.

Three entry points to the same puzzle.

---

# Why frameworks?

A good framework helps us read three things at once:

1. **The growth story** — why did this country grow when it did, and stagnate when it did?
2. **The syndrome** — why do binding constraints persist? What keeps them in place even when everyone can see them?
3. **Feasibility** — which recommendations have a chance of being adopted, implemented, and sustained?

The work: understand developing countries, and produce recommendations they can act on.

## Douglass North — the violence problem

The fundamental challenge of any state: containing organized violence.

**Limited access orders** (most societies, historically) — elites restrict political and economic competition. Rents are the glue that keeps the coalition stable instead of fighting.

**Open access orders** (a handful of modern states) — impersonal rules, competitive politics, competitive markets. But these only emerge *after* the violence problem has been solved.

A bridge from Olson and Tilly to everything that follows — institutions, settlements, deals.

# A practitioner synthesis

Integrates the political-economy traditions we just walked through into a framework for practitioners — directly in line with the work of weaving political economy into growth diagnostics.

Two dimensions:

- **Power concentration**
- **Social foundation**

---

# Three blocs inside a polity

- **Leaders Bloc (LB)** — assured supporters of the current leader
- **Contingently Loyal Bloc (CLB)** — currently aligned, could realistically defect
- **Opposition Bloc (OB)** — not currently aligned

Ruling coalition = LB + CLB. Can face external opposition *and* fracture from within.

# Why it matters for growth

**Concentrated** settlements offer:

1. **Long time horizons** (stationary bandits) — more investment, more productive rent-seeking, space to build disciplined state capacity
2. **Enforcement** — can impose growth policy on losers, whether rent-seeking elites or expropriated popular classes

**Broad** social foundations push elites toward inclusive growth — perhaps slower, but more equitable.
